# Ahumma Brand Voice Guide

## Purpose

This guide turns the approved Ahumma direction into repeatable writing rules for the storefront. It is informed by Ahumma's current copy and the accessible Arami Essentials experience. Aesop-specific writing observations remain unverified because direct inspection was blocked by Cloudflare.

This is an Ahumma system, not a template for copying another brand.

## Core voice

Ahumma sounds:

- Punchy, warm, and self-assured.
- Premium without sounding corporate, clinical, or distant.
- Nigerian-rooted with global ambition.
- Specific about Black and brown skin.
- Sensory and emotionally clear.
- Culturally grounded without explaining or defending its heritage.
- Simple enough to scan quickly, memorable enough to stay with someone.

## Five writing principles

### 1. Lead with feeling, then clarify

Use the headline to create recognition or desire. Use the next line to explain the category, origin, audience, or action.

**Approved example:**

> A New Ritual for Black and Brown Skin.

> Premium body care made in Nigeria, rooted in African beauty traditions and created for Black and brown skin everywhere.

### 2. Use strong nouns and active verbs

Prefer skin, scent, butter, lather, softness, care, body, origin, texture, and ritual. Prefer made, melts, cleanses, nourishes, softens, holds, and returns.

Avoid stacked abstract nouns such as “wellness journey,” “self-care experience,” or “beauty transformation.”

### 3. Make the sensory experience concrete

Describe how a product feels, spreads, lathers, smells, or fits into the day. Sensory writing should support a verified product fact, not replace one.

> Rich, whipped, and beautifully softening.

is stronger than:

> A luxurious solution for your complete body-care needs.

### 4. State the audience with confidence

Use “Black and brown skin” when the audience or proposition needs to be clear. Do not dilute the brand into “every body,” “all skin,” or “for everyone.”

Do not force the phrase into every product sentence. The surrounding system can carry the positioning through the hero, About page, consultation, product context, and imagery.

### 5. Let ritual mean something

“Ritual” should describe a repeatable act of care: the post-shower application, the first lather, the butter warmed between palms, or five quiet minutes.

Use it less when “care,” “use,” “routine,” “lather,” or “application” is more precise.

## Copy mechanics

- Keep headlines short: usually 3–8 words.
- Keep supporting paragraphs to 1–3 short sentences before a link.
- Use sentence case for customer-facing headlines unless an existing design treatment requires otherwise.
- Use punctuation to create a pause, not decoration.
- Use em dashes sparingly.
- Avoid exclamation marks in premium commerce copy.
- Use “made in Nigeria” when origin is the point; use “Nigerian-born” when describing the brand.
- Say “African black soap” for the category and “Ara” for the Ahumma product.
- Say “Black and brown skin” for the audience; do not use “melanin-rich skin” as a substitute everywhere.
- Prefer “even-looking tone,” “softness,” and “comfort” over clinical or corrective promises.

## Words to favor

care, body, skin, softness, comfort, lather, butter, texture, scent, glow, nourish, soften, cleanse, considered, beautiful, made, rooted, modern, everyday, return, hold, enjoy, feel, Lagos, Nigeria, African heritage, Black and brown skin.

## Words to limit or avoid

fix, correct, repair, cure, erase, transform, flawless, perfect, miracle, detox, solution, treatment, anti-aging, poreless, universal, everyone, all skin types, clean beauty, non-toxic, chemical-free, empowerment, elevate, journey, indulge in your best self, self-care journey.

Use “natural” only where a product or legal claim supports it. Use “luxury” rarely; let texture, formulation, photography, and restraint create the premium feeling.

## Channel guidance

### Hero

The headline carries the feeling. The supporting line carries category, origin, heritage, and audience. Use one confident CTA.

- Headline: **A New Ritual for Black and Brown Skin.**
- Supporting line: **Premium body care made in Nigeria, rooted in African beauty traditions and created for Black and brown skin everywhere.**
- CTA: **Start my ritual**

Useful alternatives for testing, not simultaneous use:

- Shop body care — direct and commerce-led.
- Explore the collection — best when the hero needs less personal language.
- Find your ritual — best when consultation is the intended first step.

### Homepage sections

Each section should make one point:

- Philosophy: what Ahumma believes.
- Product rail: what to try first.
- Visual break: how the care feels.
- Global story: where Ahumma comes from and where it is going.
- Testimonials: evidence from real customers only.
- Newsletter: why staying close is useful.

Do not repeat the entire hero proposition in every section.

### Product cards

Use the product name, one sensory or use cue, and a clear action. Keep card copy short enough to scan beside the image.

Good pattern:

> Dream Whip
> Cloud-rich night butter for deeply cared-for skin.
> Shop Dream Whip

Only use a benefit, ingredient, scent, or skin concern when the product data supports it.

### Product pages

Follow this order:

1. Product name.
2. Sensory description.
3. Price and purchase action.
4. Three verified highlights.
5. Texture, scent, and use.
6. How to use.
7. Structured ingredients and details.
8. A relevant ritual pairing.

The page should make the product desirable before asking the customer to process technical detail.

### About and story

Use a clear sequence: belief, origin, formulation, experience, invitation. Keep the cultural context specific and contemporary.

Preferred direction:

> Proudly Nigerian. Rooted in African beauty traditions. Made for modern body care and Black and brown skin everywhere.

Avoid turning heritage into a decorative paragraph disconnected from what the products do.

### Ingredient stories

Name the ingredient. State its role in the formula or sensory experience. Link to the products that actually contain it when that data is structured and verified. A bounded, manually verified fallback is acceptable for the current known range while catalogue metadata is being completed; never extend it to an unknown product.

Do not imply that every Ahumma product contains every ingredient in the brand story. Do not describe a product as containing “avocado oil” when the verified product data says “avocado butter.”

### Consultation

Make the customer feel guided, not assessed.

Prefer:

> Tell us what your skin needs and what care should feel like.

Avoid:

> Discover your skin diagnosis and treatment plan.

The consultation offers product guidance, not medical advice. Keep the safety notice intact.

### Newsletter and popup

The footer signup is the primary relationship surface. The popup is a separate promotional moment.

Footer direction:

> Join the Ahumma Circle.
> First access to new rituals, limited releases and stories from Ahumma.
> Join us

Popup copy should earn the interruption with a specific launch, limited release, story, or offer that is actually configured. Do not make the popup sound like an error state or a replacement for the footer form.

### FAQs, errors, and checkout

Be direct, calm, and useful.

- Explain what happened.
- Say what the customer can do next.
- Avoid blame or vague reassurance.
- Do not turn operational uncertainty into a brand poem.

Good error direction:

> We couldn’t load the collection just now. Try again, or come back shortly.

## CTA system

| CTA                                    | Best use                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Start my ritual                        | Hero or guided first step            |
| Shop body care                         | Direct commerce entry                |
| Explore the collection                 | Product discovery with a softer tone |
| Find your ritual                       | Consultation route                   |
| Shop Sika / Shop Dream Whip / Shop Ara | Specific product route               |
| Explore the ingredient stories         | About ingredient section             |
| Talk through your routine              | Concern-to-consultation link         |
| Build the ritual                       | Curated product pairing section      |
| Join us                                | Footer newsletter                    |

Avoid vague CTAs such as “Learn more,” “Discover now,” or “Click here” when a more specific action is available.

## Reference patterns Ahumma can adapt

From the accessible Arami experience, Ahumma can adapt:

- Short philosophy-led statements.
- Product writing that makes everyday repetition feel desirable.
- Concern-led discovery.
- Routine and lifestyle media as a product counterpoint.
- A newsletter framed around access, launches, and stories.
- Grouped footer navigation that supports shopping, story, help, and contact.

Aesop-specific copy patterns are not recorded as verified findings in this pass.

## Copy QA checklist

Before publishing copy, ask:

- Does it sound like Ahumma rather than a generic luxury skincare brand?
- Is the Nigerian origin present where it adds meaning?
- Is Black and brown skin represented clearly where it matters?
- Is every ingredient, benefit, and operational promise verified?
- Is the sentence shorter than it needs to be?
- Can a customer understand the action immediately?
- Does “ritual” describe a real behavior?
- Does the copy avoid correction, bleaching, medical, or universal claims?
- Does the section add a new idea rather than repeat the hero?
- Is the copy supported by the image or product interaction beside it?
